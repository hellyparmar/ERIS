# The ERIS demo dataset

## What the data is
ERIS ships with a synthetic dataset for one fictional neighbourhood supermarket chain (packaged groceries, fresh produce, dairy, and household and personal care) with five outlets by default (configurable up to seven) in Mumbai, Pune, Bengaluru and Ahmedabad. It is generated by code, not collected from a real business, and every synthetic bill is marked with the data source "synthetic" and linked to a dataset provenance record (generator version, random seed, generation time and period covered).

## How it is generated
The generator simulates about two years of bills basket by basket. The number of bills per day depends on outlet size, weekday, a growth trend, season, festivals, rainfall and a ramp-up for newly opened outlets. The products in each basket depend on popularity, seasonality, temperature and rain, promotions (demand elasticity 2.0), price changes (elasticity 1.0) and companion items such as bread with butter. When a product is out of stock the shopper switches to a substitute 60% of the time, otherwise the sale is lost.

## Ground truth stored with the data
Promotions, price history, daily weather per city (with 90 days of climatology ahead), stockout events with their substitutes and labelled anomalies are stored in their own tables. The labelled anomalies are point-of-sale outages, one-off bulk orders, data-entry errors with an extra "00" in a quantity, local events and outlet closures. They are used to measure how well the anomaly detector works.

## Reproducibility
The same random seed (default 42) and end date always produce the same dataset. The number of days, outlets, the seed and the end date can be set with environment variables or with the command `python -m app.seed --days 730 --outlets 5 --seed 42`.

## Real data
Users can add real data by entering sales manually or importing CSV files. Those records are marked with the source "manual" or "import". Settings can clear all transactions to start from an empty history.
