# The ERIS demo dataset

## What the data is
ERIS ships with a synthetic dataset for one fictional casual-dining restaurant chain, Saffron Table, with five restaurants by default (configurable up to seven) in Mumbai, Pune, Bengaluru and Ahmedabad. The menu has 50 dishes and drinks in 9 categories (soups, starters, main course, breads, rice & biryani, Indo-Chinese, desserts, hot and cold beverages); the kitchens stock 38 ingredients bought from 7 suppliers, and every dish has a recipe. The data is generated by code, not collected from a real business, and every synthetic bill is marked with the data source "synthetic" and linked to a dataset provenance record (generator version, random seed, generation time and period covered).

## How it is generated
The generator simulates about two years of bills one by one. The number of bills per day depends on restaurant size, weekday (Friday to Sunday are busiest), a growth trend, season, festivals (fewer guests on Diwali night, more on New Year's Eve and Valentine's Day), rainfall and a ramp-up for newly opened restaurants. Each bill has a channel - dine-in, takeaway or delivery (rain pushes orders to delivery) - and a party size (covers). The party orders by course: mains, breads or rice with curries, starters, soups, drinks and desserts, chosen by popularity, season and weather, promotions (demand elasticity 2.0), menu price changes (elasticity 1.0) and each restaurant's appetite for non-vegetarian food, which drops during Navratri and Shravan. Ingredient use follows from the recipes. When an ingredient runs out, the dishes that need it come off the menu for the day: a guest picks a similar dish 60% of the time, otherwise the order is lost.

## Ground truth stored with the data
Promotions, menu prices and monthly ingredient costs (tomato and onion prices spike seasonally), daily weather per city (with 90 days of climatology ahead), dish outages with their substitutes and labelled anomalies are stored in their own tables. The labelled anomalies are billing-system outages, one-off corporate party orders, data-entry errors with an extra "00" in a quantity, local events (a concert or match nearby) and closures. They are used to measure how well the anomaly detector works.

## Reproducibility
The same random seed (default 42) and end date always produce the same dataset. The number of days, outlets, the seed and the end date can be set with environment variables or with the command `python -m app.seed --days 730 --outlets 5 --seed 42`.

## Real data
Users can add real data by entering bills manually or importing CSV files. Those records are marked with the source "manual" or "import". Settings can clear all transactions to start from an empty history.
